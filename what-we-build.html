Inductor Types We Build

Every design is reviewed for winding feasibility, insulation margins, thermal rise, and process stability before release to production.

By Core Geometry

  • Toroidal inductors: ferrite and powdered iron cores, wound for uniform tension and controlled fill
  • Bobbin-wound inductors: E, EE, EI, UI, RM, and PQ geometries with defined lead routing and insulation systems
  • Gapped core inductors: distributed or discrete gaps with a documented inductance control strategy
  • Laminated steel inductors: GO and NGO laminations with controlled stack height and orientation
  • Mechanically integrated magnetic assemblies: inductors designed to interface with housings, brackets, or structural elements

By Electrical Intent

  • Power inductors: energy storage designs governed by saturation margin and current density
  • Filter inductors: EMI and ripple reduction elements built for predictable impedance behavior
  • Coupled inductors: intentional leakage and coupling coefficients verified during design review
  • Chokes: common-mode and differential constructions with insulation and symmetry control
  • Application-specific inductive elements: defined by waveform shape, operating frequency, and thermal limits rather than nominal inductance alone

By Manufacturing Method

  • Precision layer-wound inductors: controlled turn placement for repeatability and insulation integrity
  • Toroidal wound inductors: manual or semi-automated winding selected by wire gauge and fill constraints
  • Multi-winding and interleaved constructions: used to manage capacitance, coupling, and thermal gradients
  • Heavy-gauge or parallel conductor builds: including litz, parallel round wire, or foil where required by loss or temperature rise

Quantitative Manufacturing Envelopes

These ranges describe where our processes are stable and repeatable. Designs outside these envelopes are reviewed for risk before quotation.

Electrical and Thermal

  • Inductance: approximately 1 µH to 100 mH, typically ±5–10% tolerance (tighter by agreement with defined test method)
  • DC current: up to approximately 50 A continuous, governed by conductor size and thermal rise limits
  • Saturation margin: core selection targets a minimum 20% margin at maximum operating current unless otherwise specified
  • Temperature rise: typically designed for ≤40–60 °C rise at rated current under defined ambient and airflow conditions

Conductors and Windings

  • Wire gauge: approximately AWG 32 to AWG 10 for single conductors; parallel wire or litz used where required
  • Winding layers: single to multi-layer constructions limited by insulation system and voltage stress
  • Interleaving: applied where coupling, leakage, or EMI performance requires it and validated before release

Cores and Materials

  • Core materials: ferrite, powdered iron, laminated silicon steel (GO and NGO)
  • Operating frequency: DC to approximately 500 kHz, constrained by core loss, winding loss, and thermal budget
  • Gap control: discrete or distributed gaps selected for inductance stability at production volume

Mechanical and Production

  • Typical unit size: handheld to palm-sized magnetic assemblies; larger units reviewed for handling and fixturing
  • Production volumes: pilot builds from tens to hundreds; sustained production from thousands to tens of thousands with a locked process
  • Repeatability controls: defined winding instructions, incoming material controls, and in-process plus final electrical verification
