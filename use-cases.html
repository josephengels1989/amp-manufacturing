Use Cases for Custom Magnetic Components

Custom inductors and transformers serve critical roles across power electronics, sensing, and signal processing. These six applications represent the most common reasons engineers specify custom magnetic components.

Use Case 1

Current Sensing

Current transformers measure AC current flowing through a conductor by inducing a proportional current in a secondary winding. The primary conductor passes through the center of a toroidal core. The secondary winding, wrapped around the core, outputs a scaled-down current that is safe to measure with standard instrumentation.

Accuracy depends on the core material. High-permeability cores like 1J85 (Permalloy) minimize magnetizing current error and produce linear output across a wide current range. Our current sensing toroids use 1J85 cores with permeability above 100,000, wound with 750 to 1,500 turns of 34 AWG wire.

Use Case 2

Power Supply Filtering

Every AC/DC power supply includes filter inductors to smooth the rectified output voltage. Filter chokes block high-frequency ripple while passing DC current to the load. The inductor stores energy during the switching on-time and releases it during the off-time, reducing voltage variation at the output.

Core material depends on the switching frequency. Ferrite cores excel in switching power supplies operating at 50 kHz to 500 kHz, where their low core loss keeps efficiency high. Silicon-iron cores are used in line-frequency filter chokes (50/60 Hz) where high saturation flux density is needed to handle large DC currents.

Ferrite or Silicon-Iron
Wire
18 to 26 AWG copper
Turns
50 to 750
Key Parameter
Low DCR, high saturation

Use Case 3

EMI Suppression

Electromagnetic interference (EMI) is unwanted electrical noise that travels through power lines and signal cables. Common mode chokes and EMI filter inductors block this noise by presenting high impedance to high-frequency signals while allowing the desired DC or low-frequency power to pass through unimpeded.

A common mode choke consists of two windings on a single toroidal core, wound in opposite directions. Normal differential-mode current (the power the circuit needs) produces opposing magnetic fields that cancel out, so the choke presents zero impedance to normal operation. Common-mode noise (both lines carrying noise in the same direction) sees the full inductance of both windings, and the choke blocks it.

Typical Specifications

Core Material
Ferrite (MnZn or NiZn)
Wire
26 to 34 AWG copper
Winding
Bifilar (two wires wound together)
Frequency Target
100 kHz to 30 MHz

Use Case 4

Energy Storage Inductors

In DC/DC converter topologies (buck, boost, buck-boost), the inductor is the primary energy storage element. During the switch on-time, current ramps up and energy is stored in the magnetic field. During the off-time, the stored energy is released to the output. This charge-discharge cycle happens at the switching frequency, typically 100 kHz to 1 MHz.

The critical design parameter is the saturation current. If the peak inductor current causes the core to saturate, inductance drops sharply, current spikes, and the converter loses regulation. Core material selection must ensure that the peak flux density stays well below the saturation limit across all operating conditions, including startup transients and load steps.

Typical Specifications

Core Material
Ferrite (gapped)
Wire
18 to 26 AWG copper
Key Parameter
Saturation current, energy storage (1/2 LI²)
Frequency
100 kHz to 1 MHz

Use Case 5

Signal Isolation

Isolation transformers provide galvanic separation between two circuits. There is no direct electrical connection between the primary and secondary windings. Energy transfers solely through the magnetic field in the core. This isolation protects sensitive circuits from ground loops, voltage spikes, and fault currents.

In medical devices, isolation transformers are a safety requirement. They prevent hazardous leakage current from reaching the patient. In industrial controls, isolation prevents ground-loop-induced noise from corrupting measurement signals. The isolation barrier must withstand the specified hi-pot voltage (typically 1,500 to 4,000 VAC) without breakdown.

Use Case 6

Motor Drive Filtering

Variable frequency drives (VFDs) control motor speed by synthesizing an AC output from high-frequency pulse-width-modulated (PWM) switching. The output waveform contains significant high-frequency harmonics that can damage motor insulation, cause bearing currents, generate audible noise, and produce electromagnetic interference.

Output chokes (also called dV/dt filters or load reactors) are placed between the VFD and the motor. These inductors slow down the voltage rise time of the PWM pulses, reducing peak voltage stress on the motor windings. They also filter harmonic content, producing a smoother current waveform that reduces motor heating and acoustic noise.

Typical Specifications

Core Material
Silicon-Iron (high saturation)
Wire
18 AWG copper (heavy gauge)
Current Rating
