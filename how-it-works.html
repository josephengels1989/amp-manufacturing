How We Work

Drawing to doorstep. Here's how it works.

A clear process built around accountability, not red tape. From initial review to final shipment, you work with the same team under the same standards.

The Process

Conversation to delivery

1. Initial Conversation

Tell us what you need: specs, quantities, timeline, application. We'll ask the right questions to understand the full scope and determine if we're the right fit for the job.

2. Drawing Review

Send us your drawings, spec sheets, or reference documentation. We review for manufacturability, flag potential issues with tolerances or materials, and come back with technical questions — not generic follow-ups.

3. Quote & Timeline

Detailed quote with realistic lead times, typically within 2–3 business days. Line-item pricing, no hidden fees. The number we quote is the number you pay.

4. Material Sourcing

We source cores, wire, and insulation materials through vetted suppliers. If your application requires a specific core manufacturer or wire type, we coordinate with them directly. You deal with us, not a supply chain.

5. Production

Winding, assembly, and in-process verification. We update you on progress and flag anything that comes up — material delays, spec clarifications, schedule changes. You'll never have to chase us for a status update.

6. Testing & Verification

100% tested — inductance, DCR, hi-pot, and any additional parameters you specify. Not spot checks, not sampling. Every unit verified against your specification before it leaves our facility.

7. Delivery

Shipped with full documentation: test reports, certificates of conformance, traceability records. Your specs stay on file — reorders are seamless.

What We Need From You

To get started

Drawings or specifications: CAD files, schematics, spec sheets, or documentation from a previous supplier. We'll work with whatever you have.

Quantity estimates: How many units per month? Are quantities consistent or variable?

Timeline: When do you need them? Any recurring deadlines we should know about?

Application context: What system does this go into? Frequency range, operating environment, regulatory requirements. The more we understand the application, the better we can spec the component.
