How We Evaluate Inductor Designs for Production

This page describes how Ampersand evaluates inductor designs for manufacturability.

It is intended for engineering teams responsible for products in or near release, not exploratory design work.

Our evaluation process exists to answer a single question:

Can this design be built repeatedly, without drift, at the required volume and duration?

What This Review Is and Is Not

This review is:

  • A manufacturing feasibility assessment
  • A risk identification step before samples or RFQ
  • A way to surface constraints early

This review is not:

  • A consulting design service
  • A speculative prototype exercise
  • A guarantee of acceptance without revision

Designs that cannot be stabilized for production are identified early and revised or declined.

Step 1: Core Geometry & Mechanical Feasibility

We begin by confirming that the proposed geometry can be built and assembled repeatedly.

We review:

  • Core shape and material (ferrite, powdered iron, laminated steel)
  • Gapping method (distributed vs discrete, tolerance sensitivity)
  • Mechanical interfaces (bobbin fit, clamping, housing constraints)
  • Mounting orientation and stress points

At this stage, we are verifying that the geometry is manufacturable without operator-dependent variation.

If a design depends on manual tuning or undefined mechanical compliance, we flag it for discussion with you.

Step 2: Winding Method & Conductor Selection

Next, we evaluate whether the winding can be executed consistently under production conditions.

We assess:

  • Required winding method (layered, toroidal, interleaved, multi-winding)
  • Wire gauge, insulation type, and availability
  • Turn density and fill margin
  • Termination method and strain relief

Winding approaches are selected for repeatability under thermal and electrical load.

Step 3: Insulation, Dielectric, and Thermal Margins

We review electrical compliance in parallel with long-term reliability.

We look at:

  • Insulation system and temperature class
  • Creepage and clearance margins
  • Expected thermal rise under continuous operation
  • Interaction between winding density and heat dissipation

Margins are evaluated against real operating conditions rather than lab-only scenarios.

If thermal or dielectric margins are too narrow to tolerate production variation, we recommend revisions before proceeding.

Step 4: Material Availability & Supply Stability

We do not approve a design without confirming that materials can be sourced affordably and consistently over time.

We review:

  • Core material availability and alternates
  • Magnet wire types and insulation systems
  • Sensitivity to supplier or lot variation

Acceptable material equivalents are identified in advance to reduce future disruption.

Designs that rely on fragile or single-source materials are flagged early, and we discuss this with you.

Step 5: Process Definition & Inspection Strategy

Before a design progresses, we confirm that it can be supported by a defined process.

This includes:

  • Clear build sequence
  • Identifiable inspection points
  • Test coverage aligned with risk
  • Traceability requirements by program

Documentation is one of our leading values. If a process cannot be documented it cannot be scaled.

Step 6: Volume, Duration, and Change Control

Finally, we review the intended production context.

We confirm:

  • Expected monthly volume range
  • Program duration
  • Change tolerance over time
  • Requirements for revision control and notification

Designs intended for sustained production are evaluated differently than short-run builds.

Outcomes of the Evaluation

After review, one of the following paths is defined:

  • Proceed to pilot build
  • Proceed with recommended design adjustments
  • Defer pending clarified constraints
  • Decline due to manufacturability risk

Each outcome is defined through direct technical discussion. We review findings together, resolve open questions, and agree on the path forward before work proceeds.

What to Submit for Review

To initiate an evaluation, teams typically send us an email with:

  • Electrical specification or drawing
  • Mechanical drawing (PDF or STEP)
  • Target volumes and timeline
  • Known environmental or regulatory constraints

A Note on Non-Standard Assemblies

We evaluate non-standard geometries, hybrid cores, and mechanically integrated assemblies on a program basis. We would be honored to have a look at your drawings and confer with you.
