Our Story

How we got here

We had a contact through our network who'd been in the inductor business for 40 years. He needed parts built and was willing to show us how the industry worked: what kind of equipment and facility space we'd need and what most buyers expect from a new supplier.

We set up a winding facility in Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam. Climate-controlled, clean, purpose-built for fine wire work. We chose Vietnam because it has a strong relationship to the United States, a technical workforce, and a deep labor pool of young engineers. Over the course of two years and many mistakes and pratfalls, the right people came together. Two years in, legally registered in both countries, team in place, we went looking for our first client.

Our first client was NK Technologies in San Jose, which specializes in current sensors for industrial power monitoring. We approached them cold and got a lunch meeting with their buyer. She liked us enough to bring her CTO to a second lunch. We sat down across the table from two experienced manufacturing executives and told them the truth: one of us is a university professor, the other a psychotherapist, and over the past two years we built an inductor company from scratch.

They laughed. Then they gave us their drawings.

We built their samples: exact turn counts, wire gauges, DCR tolerances, lead lengths down to the hundredth of an inch. Those samples passed every test. NK placed their first production order, and we've been supplying them since.

At this point in the story, it should be said that the hardest part of our first order wasn't winding. It was shipping. Coordinating international freight, setting up forwarders (good ones, most aren't) for the first time, figuring out customs timing and (how could we forget) efficiently handling shifting tariff law.

Today we review the test data on every batch personally. We video call our winding floor during production runs. Every shipment leaves with our signatures on it and our forwarders know us by our first names. That's how two outsiders to this industry make sure everything going out the door is right.
