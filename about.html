Our Story

How we got here

We had a contact through our network who'd been in the inductor business for 40 years. He needed parts built and was willing to show us how the industry worked: what kind of equipment we'd need, what kind of facility space, and what buyers expect from a supplier.

We set up a winding facility in Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam. Climate-controlled, clean, purpose-built for fine wire work. Vietnam has a strong technical workforce and a deep labor pool of young engineers looking to build something. We found the right people fast.

We got the company legally registered in Vietnam and the United States. Built our own supply chain from scratch: core vendors, magnet wire suppliers, freight forwarders. None of that came from a playbook.

Our first client was NK Technologies in San Jose. They manufacture current sensors for industrial power monitoring. We approached them cold and got a lunch meeting with their buyer. She liked us enough to bring her CTO to a second lunch. We sat down across the table from two experienced manufacturing executives and told them the truth: one of us is a university professor, the other is a psychotherapist, and we just started an inductor company.

They laughed. Then they gave us their drawings.

We built the samples. Toroidal coils wound to their exact specifications: turn counts, wire gauges, DCR tolerances, lead lengths down to the hundredth of an inch. The samples passed every test. NK placed their first production order, and we've been supplying them since.

The hardest part of that first order had nothing to do with winding. It was shipping. Coordinating international freight, setting up forwarders, figuring out customs timing. The inductors were built right. Getting them to San Jose on schedule took some figuring out. We got it done, and every order since has moved faster.

Today we review the test data on every batch personally. We video call our winding floor during production runs. We sign off on every shipment before it leaves the facility. That's how two people who came from outside this industry make sure everything going out the door is right.
