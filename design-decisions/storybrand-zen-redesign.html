StoryBrand Wireframe + Zen Visual Theme

Decision date: 2026-05-03 · Commit: ca3fadf

The Decision

The homepage was rebuilt around Donald Miller's StoryBrand 7-part framework (the customer is the hero, Ampersand is the guide, single call-to-action). The old homepage had multiple competing CTAs and felt like a generic capabilities brochure. The new structure runs Problem → Stakes → Guide → Plan → Identity Transformation → one Final CTA.

Visual language switched from dark industrial to a light "zen" palette: warm washi-paper background, sumi-ink text, muted sage accent, Cormorant Garamond serif headings, generous whitespace. The logo mark changed from the literal ampersand & to an ensō — the open Zen circle painted in one breath with a sumi brush. Implemented as a 5-layer SVG arc that tapers from thin tip to thick belly to thin tail.

Why

The hero copy now opens with the customer's pain rather than our capabilities:

"Our competitors don't respect you. They disrespect you in both pricing and communication."

The Bob Nguyen quote and the NK Technologies growth story (5,000 cores → half their catalog in 12 months) carry the authority. Three contact methods (text, email, live call) collapse into a single ask: schedule a call with our intake coordinator.

Why Zen + Ensō

Most competitors in the inductor space use a hard, technical, dark, blue-and-grey aesthetic. A warm, contemplative palette and a hand-painted brushstroke logo signal craft, attention, and care — the same qualities that distinguish Ampersand's communication and quality from transactional suppliers. The ensō (open circle, brushstroke) maps directly to the "wholeness, but never closed" identity of a partner who keeps showing up.
